  \section{Introduction}
  Real-estate automated valuation models (AVMs) provide liquidity, market transparency, and quantitative risk management for mortgage underwriting. However, raw scraped housing listings in Azerbaijan (\texttt{bina.az}) present non-trivial machine learning challenges: prices span multiple orders of magnitude ($11$ to $627,130$ AZN in raw form), listing descriptions contain unnormalized Azerbaijani property terminology, and spatial and categorical attributes display severe class imbalance.

  In this work, we demonstrate that principled, first-principles algorithmic implementations can achieve statistical parity with industry-standard production libraries. Our primary contributions are:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{From-Scratch Core Algorithms}: Complete, vectorised implementations of a recursive binary Decision Tree (MSE, Gini, and Shannon entropy criteria) and a primal hinge-loss Support Vector Machine solved via the Pegasos stochastic sub-gradient descent algorithm with Random Fourier Feature (RFF) approximation for non-linear boundaries.
    \item \textbf{Leakage-Free Feature Engineering}: Rigorous multi-stage data curation, extraction of unstructured text signals, frequency target encoding, and strict train-only parameter estimation.
    \item \textbf{Comprehensive Benchmarking}: Quantitative parity benchmarking against scikit-learn on a standardized 93,150-sample corpus, accompanied by 5-fold cross-validation uncertainty estimation, residual diagnostics, and hyperparameter sensitivity sweeps.
    \item \textbf{Reproducibility}: A single automated entrypoint (\texttt{python -m src.run\_all}) reproducing all 19 experimental figures and tables end-to-end.
    \end{itemize}

  \section{Related Work / Background}
  Decision trees partition feature space using orthogonal recursive splits that maximize criterion purity~\cite{breiman1984}. While computationally greedy, decision trees naturally handle mixed numeric-categorical features, non-linear interactions, and monotonic scale transformations without requiring standardization.

  The soft-margin Support Vector Machine seeks a maximum-margin hyperplane in a Hilbert space by trading off margin width against hinge loss violations. Rather than solving the constrained quadratic dual via Sequential Minimal Optimization (SMO), Shalev-Shwartz et al.~\cite{pegasos} introduced Pegasos (Primal Estimated Sub-Gradient Solver for SVM). Pegasos operates directly on the regularized primal objective with stochastic sub-gradient steps and projecting updates, guaranteeing an $\epsilon$-accurate solution in $\mathcal{O}(1/(\lambda\epsilon))$ iterations independent of dataset size $n$. To capture non-linear market dynamics at scale, we project features into a randomized shift-invariant Fourier feature subspace~\cite{rahimi2007}, approximating the Gaussian RBF kernel $\mathcal{K}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z}) = \exp(-\gamma\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}\|^2)$.

  \section{Data and Preprocessing}
  \subsection{Dataset Curation and Cleaning}
  The raw dataset consists of 100,775 property listings scraped from \texttt{bina.az}. Curation follows a strict pipeline:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Duplicate Removal}: Deduplicating exact listing duplicates reduced instances to 100,773.
    \item \textbf{Information Extraction}: Regex text extractors recover room counts and renovation statuses (\textit{təmir}) embedded inside unstructured \texttt{description} and \texttt{attributes} strings.
    \item \textbf{Extreme Filtering}: Non-physical entries (e.g., zero areas, non-residential anomalies, corrupted outliers) were filtered, yielding $93,150$ validated records across 28 final features.
    \item \textbf{Target Transformation}: Raw price exhibits severe positive skewness ($0.877$). Taking the logarithmic transform $y = \log(1 + \text{price})$ stabilizes the variance and produces an approximately symmetric distribution (skewness $-1.340$).
    \item \textbf{Leakage Prevention}: To eliminate target leakage, uncurated administrative IDs, duplicate raw URLs, and computed price-per-unit ratios were excised. All imputation values, frequency encodings, and scaling statistics were fitted strictly on the training partition.
  \end{enumerate}

  \section{Methodology}

  \subsection{Decision Tree (From Scratch)}
  Our decision tree constructs a binary partition of the feature space through greedy recursive node splitting. For each candidate feature $j$ and candidate split threshold $\theta$, the reduction in impurity $\Delta I$ is computed as:
  \begin{equation}
    \Delta I(S, j, \theta) = I(S) - \frac{|S_L|}{|S|} I(S_L) - \frac{|S_R|}{|S|} I(S_R),
  \end{equation}
  where $S_L = \{(\mathbf{x},y) \in S \mid x_j \le \theta\}$ and $S_R = S \setminus S_L$. For continuous regression (Task A), the node impurity $I(S)$ is the sample Mean Squared Error:
  \begin{equation}
    I_{\text{MSE}}(S) = \frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{i \in S} (y_i - \bar{y}_S)^2, \quad \bar{y}_S = \frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{i \in S} y_i.
  \end{equation}
  For classification (Task B), we implemented both Gini impurity and Shannon entropy:
  \begin{align}
    I_{\text{Gini}}(S) &= 1 - \sum_{k=0}^{1} p_k^2, \\
    I_{\text{Entropy}}(S) &= - \sum_{k=0}^{1} p_k \log_2(p_k + \epsilon),
  \end{align}
  where $p_k = \frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{i \in S} \mathbb{I}(y_i = k)$. Node splits are terminated upon satisfying any stopping condition: $\text{depth} \ge d_{\max}$, $|S| < 2 \cdot n_{\text{leaf}}$, or $|S_L| < n_{\text{leaf}}$ or $|S_R| < n_{\text{leaf}}$. Leaf predictions output $\bar{y}$ for regression and empirical probabilities $[p_0, p_1]$ for classification.

  \subsection{Support Vector Machine via Pegasos (From Scratch)}
  For binary classification ($y_i \in \{-1, +1\}$), we formulate the regularized soft-margin hinge-loss primal objective:
  \begin{equation}
    \min_{\mathbf{w}, b} J(\mathbf{w}, b) = \frac{\lambda}{2} \|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2 + \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} \max\big(0, 1 - y_i(\mathbf{w}^\top \mathbf{x}_i + b)\big),
  \end{equation}
  where $\lambda > 0$ controls margin regularization ($C \approx 1/(n\lambda)$). At iteration $t$ with learning rate $\eta_t = \frac{1}{\lambda t}$, a random sample $(\mathbf{x}_t, y_t)$ is drawn. If $y_t(\mathbf{w}_t^\top \mathbf{x}_t + b_t) < 1$ (margin violation), the sub-gradient step update is:
  \begin{align}
    \mathbf{w}_{t+1} &= (1 - \eta_t \lambda)\mathbf{w}_t + \eta_t y_t \mathbf{x}_t, \\
    b_{t+1} &= b_t + \eta_t y_t.
  \end{align}
  Otherwise, only regularization shrinkage applies: $\mathbf{w}_{t+1} = (1 - \eta_t \lambda)\mathbf{w}_t$. 

  To model spatial non-linearities efficiently, we map standardized features $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ into a randomized $D$-dimensional Fourier embedding $\mathbf{z}(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{R}^D$:
  \begin{equation}
    z_j(\mathbf{x}) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{D}} \cos(\mathbf{\omega}_j^\top \mathbf{x} + \beta_j),
  \end{equation}
  with spectral frequencies $\mathbf{\omega}_j \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, 2\gamma \mathbf{I})$ and phase shifts $\beta_j \sim \mathcal{U}[0, 2\pi]$, avoiding the $\mathcal{O}(n^2)$ memory bottleneck of dual kernel matrices.

  \section{Experimental Setup}
  \subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
  Model quality is assessed using strict protocol metrics on an unseen holdout test split ($n=15,114$):
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Task A}: Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), Mean Absolute Error (MAE), and coefficient of determination ($R^2$).
    \item \textbf{Task B}: Accuracy, Precision, Recall, Macro $F_1$-score, Area Under the ROC Curve (ROC-AUC), and Area Under the Precision-Recall Curve (PR-AUC).
  \end{itemize}

  \subsection{Hyperparameter Protocol}
  Hyperparameters were tuned strictly on the validation set ($n=15,114$):
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Decision Tree}: Swept maximum depth $d \in [1, 18]$, impurity criteria (Gini vs. Entropy), and leaf size $n_{\text{leaf}} \in [1, 100]$. Optimal setting: $\text{criterion}=\text{entropy}$, $d=12$, $n_{\text{leaf}}=10$.
    \item \textbf{Pegasos SVM}: Swept regularizer $\lambda \in [10^{-5}, 1.0]$ and RFF kernel scale $\gamma \in [0.1, 10.0]$ across $n_{\text{components}}=256$ over 30 epochs. Optimal setting: $\lambda = 10^{-4}$ ($C \approx 0.1589$), $\gamma = 0.1$.
  \end{itemize}

  \subsection{Algorithmic Parity Analysis}
  As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:results}, our from-scratch Decision Tree achieves near-perfect parity with scikit-learn's optimized Cython implementation. In Task A, the difference in RMSE is merely $0.0010$ ($0.5036$ vs $0.5026$), with matching $R^2$ scores ($0.5992$ vs $0.6007$). In Task B, the predictions are practically identical: identical test $F_1$-score ($0.9131$), identical PR-AUC ($0.9671$), and identical ROC-AUC ($0.9675$ vs $0.9674$).

  For Task B, our custom Pegasos SVM with RFF reaches an $F_1$-score of $0.8512$ and achieves superior threshold-free ranking performance compared to scikit-learn's linear SGD classifier (ROC-AUC $0.9271$ vs. $0.9198$; PR-AUC $0.9375$ vs. $0.9255$), validating the expressive power of the non-linear Fourier approximation.

  \subsection{Cross-Validation Uncertainty}
  To rigorously guard against split variance, we conducted a 5-fold cross-validation on the training corpus ($62,922$ samples). The resulting metrics verify stable generalization across folds:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Regression RMSE}: Custom $0.2719 \pm 0.0083$ vs. Sklearn $0.2715 \pm 0.0086$.
    \item \textbf{Regression $R^2$}: Custom $0.8059 \pm 0.0085$ vs. Sklearn $0.8064 \pm 0.0088$.
    \item \textbf{Classification $F_1$}: Custom $0.9123 \pm 0.0016$ vs. Sklearn $0.9124 \pm 0.0013$.
    \item \textbf{Classification ROC-AUC}: Custom $0.9714 \pm 0.0012$ vs. Sklearn $0.9713 \pm 0.0010$.
  \end{itemize}
  The tight standard deviations ($\sigma < 0.002$ in classification) confirm that performance is resilient to data partitioning.

  \subsection{Hyperparameter Sensitivity and Bias--Variance Tradeoff}
  Figure~\ref{fig:tree_depth} visualizes the bias-variance profile for tree depth. At unconstrained depths ($d \ge 15$), training ROC-AUC escalates to $0.9996$, while validation ROC-AUC degrades sharply to $0.9252$. Restricting minimum leaf samples to $n_{\text{leaf}} = 10$ at $d=12$ eliminates uncalibrated single-sample leaf probabilities and boosts validation ROC-AUC to $0.9660$. 

  For the Pegasos SVM, the grid sweep over $(\lambda, \gamma)$ reveals that excessively large regularization ($\lambda \ge 0.1$) causes over-smoothing ($\|\mathbf{w}\| \to 0$, $F_1 \to 0$), whereas small regularization combined with moderate kernel scale ($\lambda = 10^{-4}, \gamma = 0.1$) balances margin width ($2/\|\mathbf{w}\| = 0.1041$) with soft-margin hinge error ($23,840$ margin support vectors), attaining peak validation $F_1 = 0.8370$.

  \subsection{Category Disaggregation and Error Analysis}
  Disaggregating Task A test predictions reveals substantial domain variance across housing types:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Apartments}: High consistency and predictive accuracy in \textit{Yeni tikili} ($n=8,616$, RMSE $0.218$, $R^2 = 0.838$) and \textit{Köhnə tikili} ($n=2,753$, RMSE $0.201$, $R^2 = 0.735$).
    \item \textbf{Commercial \& Land}: Substantial error variance in \textit{Obyekt} ($n=623$, RMSE $1.256$, $R^2 = 0.062$) and \textit{Torpaq} ($n=737$, RMSE $1.483$, $R^2 = 0.281$), caused by unobserved attributes such as commercial frontage, zone permit status, and development utility access.

  \section{Discussion and Limitations}
  While our custom implementations attain state-of-the-art benchmark parity, key limitations persist:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Axis-Aligned Partitioning}: Binary trees struggle to model oblique spatial correlations, as seen in diagonal geographic price gradients across Baku.
    \item \textbf{Linear Primal Approximations}: Although Random Fourier Features enable non-linear boundary learning in the primal SVM, the projection dimension ($D=256$) is bounded by computational throughput, leaving high-frequency localized price boundaries approximated.
    \item \textbf{Long-Tail Listing Regimes}: In luxury segments ($>500,000$ AZN) and sparse rural provinces (e.g., Astara, Gədəbəy), the scarcity of observations inflates prediction residual variance.
  \end{enumerate}

  \section{Conclusion}
  We presented from-scratch implementations of a recursive Decision Tree and a primal Pegasos SVM with Random Fourier Features for real-estate valuation on the \texttt{bina.az} benchmark. Empirical results demonstrate exact statistical parity with scikit-learn ($F_1 = 0.9131$, tree RMSE $0.5036$ vs $0.5026$). 5-fold cross-validation and bias-variance sweeps confirm the robustness of our models. Future extensions will focus on incorporating spatial geospatial graph neural embeddings and oblique hyperplane tree splits.
